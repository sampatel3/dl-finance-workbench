How it works

Code decides what is true; the model only phrases it

The figures are deterministic. What a model is allowed to do with them is bounded, and the boundary is the point of the design rather than a caveat on it.

Every figure is computed, never generated. A model phrases a bounded commentary and answers from governed tools; it never invents a number, changes an assumption, approves a pack or posts a journal. Those four are the whole list, and each is refused in code rather than discouraged in a prompt.
The evidence chain is the same object as the answer. Movement, drivers, formula, rows and source vintage hang off the figure itself, so a controller asking where a number came from is reading the thing that produced it rather than a reconstruction of it.
The wording check is a word list rather than an understanding. It catches a forecast phrased as a fact and a superlative dressed as a finding; a sentence that implies a cause without using one of the listed words reaches the reader unchanged. That is a named gap, not a solved one.

What fits inside it
  • Five finance leaders — a group CFO, two controllers, two FP&A leads — each asked the same two questions, written down before the first call.
  • The workbench at its own URL, driven by them rather than shown to them.
  • One page of answers, naming who said what.
Not in it, on purpose
  • No build. Five people seeing five different products is five answers to five different questions.
  • No real ledger, not one row. That is what makes the week need nobody’s approval.
  • No pricing. That is a different conversation with a different person.
The appetite is the constraint, not an estimate of the work: five hours is what this is worth before anybody outside the team has said the pack-versus-rebuild gap is a cost they carry. If the week needs a sixth hour it has already failed its own test.

The decision

Take the week, and decide again on Friday

Five conversations with finance leaders, each asked the same two questions: what does assembling the monthly pack cost you, and what does it cost when somebody rebuilds a figure and gets a different answer.

Who approves it

Whoever holds this team’s time. No budget, no data access, no third party — five conversations need none of the three, so this starts on the day it is read.

A yes, if

Yes, if the five are people we already have a relationship with. Cold outreach turns a five-hour week into a three-week one and the appetite does not cover it.

What a go fixes, and what it does not

It closes value and starts on usability. Viability stays open either way — nothing here goes near a real ledger, so nothing here can settle it.

If none of the five says the gap is a cost they carry, this stops on Friday — written into the gate rather than left to whoever gets tired of it first. The unwelcome consequence of a go is worth stating plainly: a week spent asking is a week the build does not move.
