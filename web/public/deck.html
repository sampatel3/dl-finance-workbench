The Problem

The evidence gap

A figure reaches a board pack with nothing attached to it. The number is there, and the working that produced it is somewhere else — in a model on somebody's machine, in a query nobody kept, in a version of the forecast that has since moved. The gap is not accuracy. It is that the pack cannot show its own reasoning when it is asked.

An executive is shown a figure, an analyst rebuilds it, a controller evidences it — and nothing forces the three to be the same computation.
The comparator a movement is measured against is usually a spreadsheet somebody keeps, so the baseline moves without a record of having moved.
A pack that cannot say which ledgers had closed when it was cut has already misled somebody, without a single figure being wrong.
Every fault here is one a controller in this group has had to answer for in person. None of them is a failure of the ledger; each is a failure to keep what the ledger was read through.

The drill reaches the rows, their measure, their period and their vintage — which load of the ledger a row was read from, since a correction arrives as a new load naming the one it restates rather than overwriting what was there. The comparator is named on the figure, and nothing is rounded before it is compared: the £12.4m and the £11.8m are rounded for the page, while the £618k and the +5.2% are computed on the figures behind them. For this to hold on your own data, the comparator has to be a versioned artefact rather than a spreadsheet somebody keeps — the first thing the hour finds out.

Roadmap

Twelve weeks, with a paid exit at week three

Evidence before build, and a stop before the build is commissioned — twelve weeks from one workflow to live operation, with the exit at week three and discovery the only thing paid for by then.

01 / Weeks 1–3
Discovery and data audit

One workflow, its baseline, its data, who owns it. Hard stop here: if data, value or path is not viable the work ends, the client pays for discovery alone and keeps the data-quality assessment, the baseline and the scope — whoever builds it.

02 / Weeks 4–8
Build and evaluate

The product against the client’s own data, with a back-test and an accuracy comparison rather than a demonstration — working software, and the evidence it was measured by.

03 / Weeks 9–11
Integrate and deploy

Production systems, permissions, controls, monitoring and the people who use it — a deployment and a runbook, not a pilot.

04 / Week 12
Validate and hand over

Live performance against the week-one baseline, knowledge transferred, ownership named.
