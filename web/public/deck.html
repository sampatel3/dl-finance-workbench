What changed

Writing the commentary got cheap; standing behind it did not

A model can phrase a month in seconds, so the scarce thing in a close is no longer the words — it is being able to say where a figure came from when somebody asks. For a group closing five ledgers, that question arrives after the pack has gone out.

What changed
seconds
to draft a month of commentary

Generated narrative is now the cheapest part of a reporting cycle, and it reads as well as the figures underneath it are true.

What did not

An executive is shown a figure, an analyst rebuilds it, a controller evidences it. Nothing forces the three to be the same computation, so they disagree.

What that leaves

The thing to look at

Any figure opens into the movement that made it

12 stops from actual to control, each one openable
5 ledgers, every row attributed to a named owner
The evidence chain is the same object as the answer. Movement, drivers, formula, rows and source vintage hang off the figure itself, so a controller asking where a number came from reads the thing that produced it rather than a reconstruction. It stops at the ledger boundary: what the workbench cannot see, it says it cannot see.

The drill reaches the rows, their measure, their period and the vintage of the source they were read from. Nothing is rounded before it is compared, and the comparator is named on the figure rather than assumed by the reader. For this to hold on the reader's own data, the comparator has to be a versioned artefact rather than a spreadsheet somebody keeps — which is the first thing an hour would find out.

How the work runs

Evidence before build, and a stop before the money

01 / Weeks 1–3
Discovery and data audit

One workflow, its baseline, its data and who owns it. Produces a data-quality assessment, the measures success is judged on, and a scope. This is where the work stops if the data is not there.

02 / Weeks 4–8
Build and evaluate

The product against the client’s own data, with a back-test and an accuracy comparison rather than a demonstration. Produces working software and the evidence it was measured by.

03 / Weeks 9–11
Integrate and deploy

Production systems, permissions, controls, monitoring and the people who use it. Produces a deployment and a runbook, not a pilot that needs a second project to become real.

04 / Week 12
Validate and hand over

Live performance measured against the week-one baseline, knowledge transferred, ownership named. Produces a live validation and a roadmap the client owns.

There is a hard stop at week three. If the data, the value or the delivery path is not viable the work ends there and the client pays for discovery alone — and keeps the data-quality assessment, the baseline and the scope, whoever builds it.

What you leave with

What it costs

An hour of two people, and no fee. If the answer is no, it is a paragraph saying which of the four blockers applies to you — and this deck and the running build stay where they are.
