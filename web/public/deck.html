The Problem

Finance has the transactions. The insight is still manual.

The accounting system records what happened; everything after that is a person. Teams export to Excel, rebuild the model, rebuild the pack and write the commentary again — and every question from a board starts the cycle over.

Management packs are rebuilt every month rather than refreshed, so the same work is paid for twelve times a year.
Forecast, variance and scenario work waits on who is available.
A figure reaches a board pack with its working somewhere else — in a query nobody kept, or a forecast version that has since moved.
None of this is a failure of the ledger. It is the work that sits after the accounting system, which no accounting system was built to do.
2/10
Why Now

Keep the accounting system. The gap is the layer above it.

Finance already owns most of this stack, and none of it should be rebuilt. What no part of it does is connect the pieces, interpret the movement and explain it to a board — which is exactly the work a governed layer on top can now take on.

Existing systems

ERP, CRM, payroll and operational systems record the transactions. They are the source of truth and they stay that way.

Already yours
Automation

OCR and workflow already handle AP and AR. BI already draws the dashboards. Planning tools already hold the budget.

Already solved
The Finance layer

A governed model of P&L, balance sheet, cash and KPIs, with the mappings, drivers and controls that make a figure defensible.

The gap
Decision

Variance, forecast, scenario and first-draft commentary — proposed by the layer, approved by the financial director. Nothing posts itself.

Where Finance stays in charge
Finance still connects, interprets and explains the information by hand. The workbench is that layer, and the demo behind these next three slides is it running on a synthetic group.

Our Product

A variance is only useful once it names what caused it.

Revenue: Forecast v6 at £11.8m to the £12.4m actual, split into its drivers — volume first at £582k, unmeasured units at £83k, the segments with no natural unit. Currency is separated out so no commercial bar carries a translation effect.

£0k unattributed — every bar named, the residual among them, and the parts sum to the movement exactly
5 ledgers, every row attributed to a named owner
The evidence chain is the same object as the answer. Movement, drivers, formula, rows and source vintage hang off the figure itself, so a controller asking where a number came from reads what produced it rather than a reconstruction. It stops at the ledger boundary: what the workbench cannot see, it says so.
5/10
Our Product

Each figure drills to the rows that made it.

The revenue figure opened: the consolidated total split into its five entities, with the intercompany elimination named as its own line.

£12.4m revenue, as the position states it
£11.8m Forecast v6, the comparator it is read against
£618k the difference, and every row behind it
+5.2%
against Forecast v6 as approved before the period opened
The drill reaches the rows, their measure, their period and their vintage — which load of the ledger a row was read from, since a correction arrives as a new load naming the one it restates rather than overwriting what was there. The comparator is named on the figure, and nothing is rounded before it is compared: the £12.4m and the £11.8m are rounded for the page, while the £618k and the +5.2% are computed on the figures behind them. For this to hold on your own data, the comparator has to be a versioned artefact rather than a spreadsheet somebody keeps — the first thing the hour finds out.
6/10
Roadmap

Twelve weeks from your ledgers to a close run on it.

The workbench is built. This is what it takes to get it running on your ledgers, and what exists at the end of each stage — measured against the baseline taken in week one, and stoppable at every step.

Weeks 1–3
Connect one close

Your last month-end loaded: the ledgers behind it, the comparator and who versions it, the mappings and the owners. You keep the data-quality assessment and the baseline whatever follows.

Weeks 4–8
Reproduce a close you signed

The position, the drivers and the evidence chain computed on your data and back-tested against closes your controllers already stood behind — naming where it reproduces them and where it does not.

Weeks 9–11
Wire it into the close

ERP and consolidation connections, permissions matching who owns which entity, the controls your audit function asks for, monitoring on the pipelines, and the controllers trained on it.

Week 12
Run a real close on it

One month-end produced through the workbench and measured against the week-one baseline, with ownership named and the surfaces that follow scoped.

Your audit function is a first-class output: model documentation, evaluation evidence, control mapping, monitoring and the operating runbook — produced by the build rather than written up afterwards.
