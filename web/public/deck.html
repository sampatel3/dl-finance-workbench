The Problem

Finance has the transactions. The insight is still manual.

The accounting system records what happened; everything after that is a person. Teams export to Excel, rebuild the model, rebuild the pack and write the commentary again — and every question from a board starts the cycle over.

Ledgers close

Five entities, four currencies. The accounting systems have every transaction, and this part is finished.

Export

Out to Excel, because nothing above the ledger can read it in place.

Rebuild

The model, the mappings, the drivers — reconstructed rather than refreshed, by whoever is available.

Rebuild again

The pack, and the commentary explaining it. Written from scratch every month.

A question

The board asks where a figure came from, and the whole cycle starts over to answer it.

Twelve times a year, the same work. None of this is a failure of the ledger — it is everything after the ledger, which no accounting system was built to do.

Why Now

Keep the accounting system. The gap is the layer above it.

Finance already owns most of this stack, and none of it should be rebuilt. What no part of it does is connect the pieces, produce the variance and the scenario, and draft what the board reads. That is the layer the workbench is. What makes it a 2026 conversation rather than a 2022 one: a model can now write that draft against a governed set of numbers and cite every figure in it back to a ledger row — which is what makes the draft usable rather than a plausible paragraph nobody can check.

Judgement

Whether the numbers are right, what to do about them, and the sign-off. A person, every month.

Our Product

A variance is only useful once it names what caused it.

Revenue: Forecast v6 at £11.8m to the £12.4m actual, split into its drivers — volume first at £582k, unmeasured units at £83k, the segments with no natural unit. Currency is separated out so no commercial bar carries a translation effect.

£0k unattributed in the bridge — every bar named, the residual among them, and the parts sum to the movement exactly. An entity table is a different object: contributions are measured at each entity rather than shared out, so they do not sum to the group and the gap is shown as its own row rather than spread
5 ledgers, every row attributed to a named owner
The evidence chain is the same object as the answer. Movement, drivers, formula, rows and source vintage hang off the figure itself, so a controller asking where a number came from reads what produced it rather than a reconstruction. It stops at the ledger boundary: what the workbench cannot see, it says so.

Our Product

Each figure drills to the rows that made it.

The revenue figure opened: the consolidated total split into its five entities, with the intercompany elimination named as its own line.

£12.4m revenue, as the position states it
£11.8m Forecast v6, the comparator it is read against
£618k the difference, and every row behind it
+5.2%
against Forecast v6 as approved before the period opened
The drill reaches the rows, their measure, their period and their vintage — which load of the ledger a row was read from, since a correction arrives as a new load naming the one it restates rather than overwriting what was there. The comparator is named on the figure, and nothing is rounded before it is compared: the £12.4m and the £11.8m are rounded for the page, while the £618k and the +5.2% are computed on the figures behind them. For this to hold on your own data, the comparator has to be a versioned artefact rather than a spreadsheet somebody keeps — the first thing the hour finds out.

Roadmap

Twelve weeks from your ledgers to a close run on it.

The twelve surfaces are built and running — on a synthetic group. What these twelve weeks build is the part that is not: extraction from your ERPs, your mappings, your permissions and your controls. There is no journal-posting path and there will not be one — the workbench reads your ledgers and never writes to them. Stoppable at the end of every stage, and each stage says what you keep.

Weeks 1–3
Connect one close, and set the baseline

Your last month-end loaded: the ledgers behind it, the comparator and who versions it, the mappings and the owners. Where it runs is settled here with your IT and audit functions — your tenant or ours, which jurisdiction, and who at Deeplight can see unpublished numbers. You keep the data-quality assessment and the baseline whatever follows.

Weeks 4–8
Reproduce a close you signed

The position, the drivers and the evidence chain computed on your data and back-tested against closes your controllers already stood behind — naming where it reproduces them and where it does not.

Weeks 9–11
Wire it into the close

ERP and consolidation connections, permissions matching who owns which entity, the controls your audit function asks for, monitoring on the pipelines, and the controllers trained on it.

Week 12
Run a real close on it

One month-end produced through the workbench, with ownership named and the surfaces that follow scoped.

Three measures, baselined in week one and re-measured in week twelve: working days from period end to a signed close · restatements after sign-off · hours spent rebuilding the model, the pack and the commentary. The threshold that counts as success is set with you in week one and written into the scope — we do not quote you a number first, because this would be our first finance close and we have no comparable one to quote from. Your audit function is a first-class output throughout: model documentation, evaluation evidence, control mapping, monitoring and the operating runbook.
