The Problem

The production gap

An enterprise can now generate anything and stand behind almost nothing. Pilots stall at the questions a risk function asks of anything that touches operations: is it integrated, evaluated, controlled, observable, operable? Most AI work cannot answer, so most AI work stays a demo.

The demo that cannot answer an auditor — impressive in a meeting, inadmissible in a control environment.
The pilot that never touches production systems, so a year of proof-of-concept proves nothing about operation.
The vendor gone at go-live — the build handed over a wall to people who did not scope it and cannot defend it.
3/18
Why Now

Three forces, crossing

The gap is not new. What is new is that all three of its drivers moved in the same two years — and the institutions that close it first set the bar the rest are audited against.

Drafting
seconds
to produce what took days

Generation became cheap, so verification became the scarce skill. The bottleneck moved from making the thing to standing behind it.

Regulation
tightening
while agents go autonomous

Supervisors formalise what AI in operations owes them exactly as enterprises push agents from copilots to actions. The two curves cross in production.

Budgets
moved
from experiments to mandates

The spend is no longer a lab line. It is an operational line with an owner, a deadline and a board asking what went live.

The same discipline that makes a figure defensible makes a delivery defensible — agents draft, people own, and everything that ships carries the evidence it shipped with.

How It Works

Five seats, one team

A delivery pod runs five seats. They are not job titles — they are the five jobs a product needs done in any ninety days, and every person spans at least two. One twelve-week engagement is one pod cycle: the Prototyper’s first evidence through the Maintainer’s handover.

01
Prototyper
Makes the future show up early — the first working answer, in days not quarters.
02
Builder
Turns demos into deployments — the distance from a working answer to a governed one.
03
Sweeper
Smaller, faster, calmer — removes what the product no longer needs to carry.
04
Grower
Finds what the market pays for, and feeds it back before the next cycle starts.
05
Maintainer
Guards the trust. Boring is a compliment on this seat.
The craft behind the seats is the 4 Ds — delegation, description, discernment, diligence. AI amplifies what a team already is, and the feeling of speed is not the same as being fast — so the pod measures its work rather than trusting how quick it felt.

Roadmap

How it runs

Evidence before build and live operation before handover, with a hard go/no-go at week three.

01 / Weeks 1–3
Discovery and data audit

Test the workflow, the value, the baseline, the data, the systems, the controls and the ownership. Ends in a go or a no-go on data, value and the delivery path.

02 / Weeks 4–8
Build and evaluate

Build the product and validate it on client data against the agreed measures. Outputs: a working product, back-test evidence, an accuracy comparison, technical documentation.

03 / Weeks 9–11
Integrate and deploy

Connect the production systems and configure permissions, controls, monitoring and users. Outputs: production deployment, a runbook, monitoring, trained users.

04 / Week 12
Validate and hand over

Measure live performance and return, transfer knowledge and confirm accountable ownership. Outputs: live validation, an ROI view, handover, an expansion roadmap.

The transformation route opens differently: an eight-week discovery led by the Quartet, starting from the proprietary 300-plus-point AI maturity evaluation — whose outcome gates which of the further playbook artefacts, from use-case portfolio to governance framework, the institution actually needs.
