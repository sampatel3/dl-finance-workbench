Verdict: no

I am the reader this deck is aimed at: a group CFO, handed the file, nobody in the room. The
headline-only read is genuinely good and the ask is cheap. I still would not take the hour on
this, because the deck contradicts itself about what the product does, never connects the pain
it opens with to the product it shows, and never once tells me what I get back for the money.

## Blocking

- **Critical** — Slide 3 ("Why Now") says two incompatible things about what the workbench does,
  on one page, and one of them has to be false.
  - The standfirst: "What no part of it does is connect the pieces, interpret the movement and
    explain it to a board — which is exactly the work a governed layer on top can now take on."
  - The diagram directly beneath it puts "Variance, forecast, scenario and the commentary a
    board reads — **still a person, every month**" in the *Decision* layer, tagged "Where the
    work is" — and places the workbench in the layer *below* that, tagged "Missing".
  - So the sentence claims the layer takes on the interpreting and explaining, and the picture
    says that work stays with a person and the workbench sits under it. This is the slide that
    carries the entire value proposition, and I cannot tell from it whether I am buying the
    thing that does the work or the thing that sits beneath the thing that does the work.
  - I would need the diagram and the standfirst to agree on which row the product occupies and
    which row stays manual, and the foot to say plainly what the product removes from a person.

- **High** — The problem the deck opens with is not the problem the deck then solves, and no
  slide bridges the two.
  - Slide 2 names the pain as a five-step manual cycle run twelve times a year: export, rebuild
    the model, rebuild the pack, rebuild the commentary.
  - Slides 4, 5 and 6 show one thing: a figure interrogated — position, bridge, drill to rows.
    Nothing in them produces a model, a pack or a commentary. The only step of the five they
    touch is the last one, "a question".
  - As it stands the deck argues "your monthly rebuild is the problem" and then demonstrates a
    lookup tool. I would need either a problem slide framed around the questions rather than the
    rebuild, or a surface that shows the pack or the model produced rather than queried.

- **High** — I am asked to spend from US$185K and twelve weeks and the deck never states a
  return, a target, or a unit of value.
  - The roadmap says a baseline is taken in week one and week twelve is "measured against the
    week-one baseline" — but the deck never says what is measured. Days off the close? Fewer
    people? Fewer restatements? Hours of board Q&A?
  - Compare the credentials slide, which is happy to quote "72h to 15min" for someone else's
    programme. Nothing equivalent is offered for this product.
  - I would need one sentence naming the measure and a plausible target, even a range, so I know
    what a successful week twelve looks like before I agree to fund it.

- **High** — There is no evidence anywhere that this product has ever touched a real ledger, and
  the deck does not admit it.
  - Every screenshot is Kestrel Industrial Group plc — declared synthetic, once, on slide 3, and
    to the deck's credit declared plainly.
  - Every credential on slide 8 is for different work: data estates and forecasting platforms at
    ADCB and EWEC. None of it is a finance close, and none of it is this product.
  - So the deck's proof section proves the team, not the workbench. There is no named finance
    function, no pilot, no reproduced close, no reference I can call.
  - The deck asks me to be the first customer without ever saying I would be the first. I would
    need it stated: either name who is running it, or say plainly that this is the first
    deployment and price the risk accordingly.

- **High** — "Twelve surfaces" is claimed once, on the title slide, and never substantiated.
  - Three are shown. The other nine are never named, listed, or dated.
  - Worse, the three shown make nearly the same argument — "every figure opens into the working
    behind it" (slide 4) and "each figure drills to the rows that made it" (slide 6) are the same
    sentence. On a skim I see one capability demonstrated three times, not a twelve-surface
    product.
  - I would need the twelve named on a single line somewhere, and the three chosen to show three
    different things.

- **High** — The commercial shape is missing. I cannot form a budget from this.
  - "From US$185K" — no upper bound, and no statement of what makes it more.
  - Nothing about what happens after week twelve: licence, subscription, hosting, support,
    per-entity or per-user cost. The workbench is described as a product I run my close on; a
    product I run my close on has a running cost, and this deck has none.
  - The roadmap says what I keep if I stop at week three (the data-quality assessment and the
    baseline). It never says what I own at week twelve — the model? the code? a tenant?
  - I would need a year-one total, or at minimum the line items that make one up.

- **High** — The section "Why Now" does not answer why now. Its headline — "Keep the accounting
  system. The gap is the layer above it." — answers *what the gap is*, which slide 2 already
  told me. The only "now" in the slide is the word itself ("can now take on"), asserted and never
  explained. Nothing dates the opportunity: no regulation, no cost pressure, no technology that
  became possible recently. If there is no reason this is a 2026 conversation rather than a 2022
  one, the section should be renamed to what it actually does.

- **High** — Nothing on security, hosting, data residency or access, for a product whose first
  act is to ingest five entities' general ledgers.
  - The roadmap mentions "the controls your audit function asks for" and "permissions matching
    who owns which entity", both inside weeks 9–11 — i.e. after I have already loaded my ledgers
    in weeks 1–3.
  - No statement of where the data sits, whose cloud, which jurisdiction, what certifications, or
    who at Deeplight can see my group's unpublished numbers. My IT and audit functions will ask
    this before I get to page two, and the deck hands me nothing to answer them with.

- **High** — The credentials are unsourced numbers about unrelated work, and there is nothing
  about the company itself.
  - "Analytical processing 72h to 15min · data quality 67% to 95% · 2,000+ users" — no
    definition of "data quality", no date, no attestation, no indication whether the client would
    confirm any of it.
  - "1,300+ models deployed inside client programmes over ten years" — "model" is undefined and
    the count is a vanity number; ten years also sits oddly against a company introduced by two
    named engagements.
  - Deeplight's own facts are entirely absent: how many people, how long trading, how many
    clients, financial standing. I am being asked to put my monthly close on a supplier and the
    deck tells me four names and two logos. A DIFC company with two Gulf references, selling to a
    £-reporting plc, also needs to say something about serving a group like mine.

- **Low** — Slides 8 and 9 contradict each other on whether I can stop. The roadmap says the
  twelve weeks are "stoppable at every step"; the Discovery card on slide 9 says Discovery is
  "the one engagement you can stop at the end of". Both cannot be true, and the one that matters
  to me is the one attached to the $185K.

- **Low** — The contents page promises sections that never arrive, and the dividers are
  misnumbered. Contents lists "Our Team" and "Engagement" under The Company; no slide carries
  either eyebrow (the engagement slide's eyebrow reads "How We Work"). Contents numbers The
  Solution 02, but its divider reads 03 — and the Company divider also reads 03. Two dividers
  numbered the same on a deck asking for six figures reads as carelessness.

- **Low** — Slide 6 buries the deck's single most important qualification. "For this to hold on
  your own data, the comparator has to be a versioned artefact rather than a spreadsheet somebody
  keeps" is the one line that tells me whether any of this works in my company — and it sits at
  the end of a hundred-word paragraph that has already been through vintages, restatement policy,
  comparator naming and rounding conventions. The rounding point in particular ("nothing is
  rounded before it is compared") is an engineering detail a CFO will not weigh, and it is
  crowding out the thing I need to read.

## Would improve

- The title slide's footer does three jobs at once — what the product is, who built them, and
  what is in the deck. The provenance sentence is the one I would cut; it reappears properly on
  slide 8.
- "Where startup speed meets enterprise scale" is the only unearned marketing phrase in an
  otherwise plain-spoken deck, and it is in bold at the top of the ask slide, where the ask
  should be. It costs the slide its best line.
- "5 ledgers, every row attributed to a named owner" is presented in the summary column as a
  product statistic; it is a fact about the synthetic demo group. So is "£0k unattributed". Both
  read as claims about my future data and are not.
- No ERP, consolidation or planning tool is named anywhere. "ERP and consolidation connections"
  in weeks 9–11 is the closest the deck comes. I cannot tell whether it connects to what I run.
- Slide 8 names four people who "scope your work and build it", but says nothing about who else
  builds, at what mix, or for how much of the twelve weeks. Four senior people cannot be the
  whole delivery team for a fixed-price build; the deck should say who else there is.
- Slide 3 says "P&L, balance sheet, cash and KPIs"; the title slide says "P&L, cash and KPIs".
  The balance sheet appears and disappears.

## What it gets right

- The headlines are claims, not topic labels. Read alone, in order, they make a real argument
  from problem to layer to product to plan to ask — which is more than most decks manage, and it
  is why I could review it at all.
- It is honest where it would be easy not to be: the group is declared synthetic, "it stops at
  the ledger boundary: what the workbench cannot see, it says so", the roadmap back-tests against
  closes my own controllers signed and promises to name where it *fails* to reproduce them, and
  the ask explicitly includes the answer "this is not worth doing".
- The ask is cheap, concrete and reversible — one hour, one bad month-end, and five named things
  I leave holding. That is the right size of first commitment and it is well specified.

## Questions it failed to pre-empt

- Has any finance function ever run a close on this? Would I be the first? The deck lets me
  assume otherwise without ever saying so.
- What does it cost in year one, all in, and what does it cost every year after?
- Where does my ledger data live, in whose infrastructure, under which jurisdiction, and who at
  Deeplight can see my unpublished numbers?
- What are the other nine surfaces, and are they built or planned?
- Does this produce my board pack and my commentary, or only let me interrogate figures someone
  else has already assembled?
- Which ERPs and consolidation systems does it connect to today, and which took how long?
- In weeks 4–8, when the workbench reproduces a close I signed and gets a different answer, what
  happens? Who decides which one is right, and does that stop the engagement?
- Am I buying a product or a consultancy build? The deck says "the workbench is built" and then
  sells me a fixed-price twelve-week engagement, and I cannot tell which side of that line I
  would be on in month thirteen.
- What is measured at week twelve, and what number would count as success?
- How big is Deeplight, how long has it existed, and has it served a UK-reporting group before?

## What I made of the product after driving it

I did not drive it, by design. My brief assigns me the one seat on this round that judges the
deck as the artefact it actually is — an argument forwarded in an inbox, read once, with nobody
to explain it. I did not open the live URL, the local app, or any application source. Everything
above is what the deck alone supports or fails to support; where I say a claim is unbacked, I
mean unbacked *in the deck*, which is the only place a reader in my position can look. If any of
these claims are in fact true of the running product, that is a fact the deck is currently
keeping to itself.
