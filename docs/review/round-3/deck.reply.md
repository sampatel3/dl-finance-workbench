Verdict: no

I know exactly what is being asked of me — "**The ask on this deck is one hour. It is not a purchase order.**" — and the deck deserves credit for that clarity. I would still not say yes, for two reasons: one slide states something its own arithmetic contradicts, the commercial slide contradicts itself about price, and the claim the deck itself nominates as the reason to have this conversation in 2026 is the one claim it never evidences.

## Blocking

- **Critical** — The bridge slide says currency is handled, then shows a bridge in which currency cannot exist. The standfirst promises "Currency is separated out so no commercial bar carries a translation effect." The bridge itself, per the alt text, is "Forecast v6 at £11.8m stepping to the £12.4m actual through volume at +£582k, price at +£22k, mix at -£19k, unmeasured units at +£83k and intercompany at -£50k, the bars summing to the movement with a residual of £0k." Those five bars sum to exactly £618k, which is exactly the movement the next slide names — "**£618k** the difference". There is no currency bar and no room for one.
  - This is a group described two slides earlier as "Five entities, four currencies", and the drill slide names Kestrel Gulf Technical Services FZ-LLC, Kestrel Europe GmbH and Kestrel Inc alongside two UK entities. A month of actuals against a forecast fixed before the period opened, across three non-sterling entities, with a translation effect of precisely zero, is not credible.
  - So either the "£0k unattributed **in the bridge** — every bar named, the residual among them, and the parts sum to the movement exactly" claim is true only because FX has been left out of the movement being explained, in which case the bridge does not reconcile the figure a CFO cares about; or the demo data has no FX movement in it, in which case the deck's flagship capability has never been exercised on the hardest part of the problem. The deck must say which, and show a currency bar with a number on it.
  - I would need to see the bridge run on a period where FX moved, with the translation line named and the residual still stated.

- **Critical** — The price is stated three different ways, and they cannot all be true. On the routes slide the Accelerate card reads "**Fixed scope, fixed price.**" and, four lines below on the same card, "**From US$185K · one workflow, live**". On the ask slide it reads "Accelerate is twelve weeks from US$185K and **would be scoped and priced after that hour**". A fixed price is not a "from"; a price fixed in advance is not one determined after a scoping hour. As written I cannot tell whether US$185K is the price, the floor, or an illustration.
  - The honest version of whichever is true — a band with what moves it, or a genuinely fixed number — is what I would need instead. "From" with no ceiling on a first-of-its-kind engagement is the number a finance function will not take to a board.

- **High** — The deck never says what I already own that this replaces, and the one line that touches it is the weakest line in the deck. The layers diagram files "Planning tools hold the budget" and "BI draws the dashboards" under the tag "Already solved", and then asserts of the layer above them "Missing · this is the workbench". A group CFO with a consolidation and CPM stack reads that as a claim their existing tools do not make. The first question any reader of this deck has — why does this not already exist in the system I bought for exactly this — is neither named nor answered anywhere in ten slides.
  - I would need one slide that names the category and says plainly what those tools do not do and this does.

- **High** — The deck's own stated reason to do this now is the one capability it never shows. Slide 3 says "What makes it a 2026 conversation rather than a 2022 one: a model can now write that draft against a governed set of numbers and cite every figure in it back to a ledger row — which is what makes the draft usable rather than a plausible paragraph nobody can check." The same slide's foot promises "What comes off a person: rebuilding the model, reconciling the pack, and **writing the first draft**."
  - Nothing in the deck shows a drafted paragraph, a citation, or a figure resolved from commentary back to a row. "Commentary" appears once, as the tenth item in the list of twelve surfaces. The three surfaces that are shown — a position, a bridge, a drill — are all things a well-built reporting stack did in 2015. The deck's differentiator is asserted and its evidence slides prove something else.

- **High** — The deck flags four material figures, three of which are bad, and then demonstrates the product twice on the only one that is good. The position slide's alt text reads "revenue £12.4m up 5.2%, gross margin 41.8% down 194bps, EBITDA £2.1m down 3.0% and cash £4.8m down 18.4%, each flagged material". Both of the next two slides open revenue: "Revenue: Forecast v6 at £11.8m to the £12.4m actual" and "The revenue figure opened".
  - The question a CFO brings to this deck is why margin fell 194bps and why cash fell 18.4% in a month revenue rose. The deck asks me to believe the evidence chain answers hard questions and only ever runs it on the easy one. Show the drill on the cash figure or the margin figure.

- **High** — I cannot tell what US$185K buys. The routes card says "One high-value production product … in twelve weeks" and "one workflow, live". The roadmap slide promises "Twelve weeks from your ledgers to a close run on it" and week 12 delivers "One month-end produced through the workbench, with ownership named and **the surfaces that follow scoped**". Twelve surfaces are advertised; weeks 4–8 build only "The position, the drivers and the evidence chain". Whether I get three surfaces or twelve for that money is never stated.
  - Related and equally unanswered: the roadmap says it is "Stoppable at the end of every stage", but no stage carries a price. If I stop after weeks 1–3 having kept "the data-quality assessment and the baseline", I do not know what I owe. An unpriced stop gate is not a stop gate.

- **High** — There is no cost of ownership past week 12. Week 12 ends with "One month-end produced through the workbench". A governed reporting layer that runs every month has a licence, a hosting bill, a support arrangement and someone operating the pipelines the deck itself promises to monitor. The deck prices twelve weeks and says nothing about year two.
  - For a reader whose job is total cost, a deck that discloses only the entry price has disclosed the smaller half.

- **High** — There is no value case, in either direction. The problem slide is entirely qualitative — "**Twelve times a year, the same work.**" — with no days, no headcount, no cost. The roadmap slide names three measures ("working days from period end to a signed close · restatements after sign-off · hours spent rebuilding the model, the pack and the commentary") and then declines to attach a number to any of them: "we do not quote you a number first, because this would be our first finance close and we have no comparable one to quote from."
  - Declining to quote your own result is the right call and I credit it below. But it does not excuse leaving the current-state side unquantified too. Nothing stops the deck stating what a close costs this group today. As it stands I am asked to weigh at least US$185K against a benefit expressed only as "the same work".

- **High** — The credentials numbers have no baseline, no method, no date and nothing to check them against. "**Analytical processing 72h to 15min · data quality 67% to 95% · 2,000+ users on self-service.**" and "**1,300+ models** … Deployed inside client programmes over ten years". Processing of what, against what workload; data quality measured by which rule set; and "model" meaning a statistical model, a deployed service or a transformation, are all unstated. No reference, no case study, no date is offered for either client.
  - The deck is admirably clear that this is "the team, not the workbench", which is the right disclosure. It should be equally clear about when this work was done and under which firm, given the CEO's prior company is named as sold in 2021.

- **High** — For a deck whose "why now" is a language model reading the numbers, it never says where the numbers go. Week 1–3 settles "your tenant or ours, which jurisdiction, and who at Deeplight can see unpublished numbers" — which covers Deeplight's people and not the model. Whose model, hosted where, trained on what, retaining what, is absent. Unpublished consolidated results of a listed group are the most controlled data a finance function holds, and this is the first question its audit and security functions will ask.

- **Low** — The "Why Now" headline answers a different question from its section. The headline is "Keep the accounting system. The gap is the layer above it." — which is *what* the gap is, not *why now*. The actual why-now answer is the fourth sentence of the standfirst. A skim reader gets no timing argument at all.

- **Low** — The materiality claim on slide 4 is undercut by the screenshot beneath it. The headline promises "a materiality policy somebody owns" and the standfirst that "What is flagged material is flagged by a versioned policy the group financial controller owns, not by whoever built the slide." The alt text then shows four measures of four "each flagged material". A policy that flags everything demonstrates nothing about the policy. Show it declining to flag something.

- **Low** — Two headlines name their subject instead of stating their claim. "One close, four measures, and a materiality policy somebody owns." is a caption for the screenshot. "A variance is only useful once it names what caused it." is a truism about variances that any competitor could print — the claim is that *this* names what caused it, and the headline does not say so.

- **Low** — The section scaffolding does not hold. The contents page promises four sections, "01 The Problem / 02 The Solution / 03 The Company / 04 The Ask", but dividers exist for only two of them. "Roadmap" is filed under 02 The Solution while the slide it points at is the twelve-week implementation plan, which is not what a reader clicking "Roadmap" expects. The deck's own build note at the top of the file still describes "Eleven numbered slides", folios that "read `n/11`", and `proof`, `team` and `close` slides — none of which exist; the folios read n/10 and those three slides are commented out.

## Would improve

- The tagline "Every figure in your close, opened to the rows that made it" is contradicted, mildly, by the honest caveat two slides later — "It stops at the ledger boundary: what the workbench cannot see, it says so." Say "every figure the ledger carries".
- The twelve-week plan promises "extraction from your ERPs" without naming a single ERP or consolidation system it already extracts from. Twelve weeks is only credible if the connectors are known quantities; as written the reader has to assume.
- The divider says "three are shown because three is what an hour holds", and the ask is also one hour. Two different hours, one word. A reader can finish the deck unsure whether the hour they are giving up is a demo or a diagnosis.
- The title slide's summary — "**In this deck:** the problem, three of the twelve surfaces running live, and the twelve weeks" — omits the ask, which is the best thing in the deck.
- "1,300+ models … under the UAE central bank (CBUAE) and inside ADGM under its regulator" implies regulatory scrutiny without saying whether anything was approved, examined or merely operated inside those perimeters.

## What it gets right

- The ask is the clearest part of the deck and unusually well-built: one hour, not a purchase order, with three named gates and five specific things I leave with, including the possibility that the answer is no. "Nothing on the previous two slides is being approved today" is the sentence that made me read the rest carefully.
- The risk disclosure is better than almost any vendor deck I read. "**You would be the first finance function to run this**, which is a risk to price rather than a detail to find later", "no figure on the three demo slides came from a real ledger", "**There is no journal-posting path and there will not be one**", and refusing to quote a benefit number because there is no comparable engagement — all of that buys real credibility.
- The numbers that are shown are internally consistent, which is rarer than it should be: the five bridge bars sum to £618k, £618k against £11.8m is the +5.2% claimed, and the five entities less the £855k elimination land on the £12.4m consolidated cell. That consistency is exactly why the missing currency bar stands out.

## Questions it failed to pre-empt

- We already run a consolidation and planning tool. What does it not do that this does?
- What does it cost to run in year two, and who operates it?
- If I stop at the end of week 3, what have I paid?
- Does US$185K deliver three surfaces or twelve on my ledgers?
- Whose model writes the commentary, where does it run, and does my unpublished consolidated result leave my tenant?
- Which ERPs and consolidation systems are already connected?
- Why is there no translation effect in a four-currency group's revenue bridge?
- Why does every demonstration open the one figure that went up, when three of the four flagged figures went down?
- What does a month-end cost this group today — days, people, hours?
- Is the hour free, and is it a demonstration or a diagnosis?

## What I made of the product after driving it

I did not drive it, by instruction. I am the deck-only reviewer for this round: I read `web/public/deck.html` as a file, including every slide's image alt text, and did not open the live URL, the local dev server or any surface of the running product. Everything above is what the deck alone supports.

That constraint is the point, and it is worth stating what it exposed. Three of my findings — the missing currency bar, the unevidenced commentary claim, and the demonstrations all opening the favourable figure — are findings a reviewer with the product in front of them would very likely not report, because they would click through to the cash surface, see the commentary working, and the deck's silence would stop registering. A reader in an inbox has no such recourse. If the running demo does answer any of these, then the deck is the thing that is wrong, not the product.
